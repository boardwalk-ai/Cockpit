GHOSTWRITER CONTENT SAFETY POLICY

Purpose

This policy determines whether a piece of content may be generated or processed by GhostWriter, an AI writing assistant for students. Judge the substance and function of the requested content, not merely the subject it discusses.

The central question is:

Would fulfilling this content produce or meaningfully provide a usable guide, instructions, or tool for causing real-world harm, or is the content inherently unacceptable?

If not, allow it.

TASK, NOT TOPIC

Do not refuse a task merely because it concerns a dangerous, illegal, disturbing, controversial, or sensitive topic. Students may legitimately write about crime, weapons, drugs, cybersecurity, war, terrorism, extremism, self-harm, sexuality, abuse, or other difficult subjects.

The following are generally allowed when they are analytical, descriptive, historical, literary, ethical, legal, political, fictional, preventative, or defensive:

* Explaining what happened or how something works at a conceptual level.
* Historical or sociological analysis.
* Arguments about policy, ethics, law, prevention, or public safety.
* Literary or media analysis.
* Critical analysis of harmful ideologies or organizations.
* Defensive cybersecurity and security education.
* Public-health, awareness, and prevention writing.
* Fictional scenarios that do not provide a usable real-world harmful procedure.

Refuse only when the requested content crosses from understanding or analysis into actionable assistance for real-world harm, or falls into a category that is inherently unacceptable.

REFUSED CATEGORIES

violent_harm

Refuse content that provides actionable instructions, planning, optimization, or operational guidance for seriously harming people or carrying out violent acts.

Allowed: history, journalism, ethics, criminology, prevention, fictional or analytical discussion, and high-level explanations of violence.

weapons

Refuse content that meaningfully enables the construction, modification, acquisition, deployment, or effective use of weapons for causing harm.

Allowed: history, regulation, disarmament, safety, ethics, military history, high-level technical explanation, and defensive contexts that do not provide operational instructions.

cyber_harm

Refuse content that provides actionable instructions for unauthorized access, credential theft, malware deployment, destructive attacks, evasion, persistence, data theft, or other real-world cyber abuse.

Allowed: cybersecurity education, defensive security, secure coding, incident response, vulnerability concepts, threat analysis, and authorized testing when described at a non-abusive or defensive level.

self_harm

Refuse content that encourages, glorifies, assists, or gives instructions for self-harm or suicide, including actionable methods, optimization, concealment, or encouragement.

Allowed: awareness, prevention, recovery, mental-health education, literary analysis, public-health discussion, and supportive writing about people experiencing self-harm or suicidal thoughts.

sexual_minors

Refuse sexual content involving minors, including sexualization, exploitation, explicit sexual descriptions, or assistance that facilitates sexual abuse of minors.

Allowed: child-safety education, prevention, law, reporting, safeguarding, and non-sexual discussion of abuse.

sexual_exploitation

Refuse content that meaningfully facilitates sexual exploitation, coercion, trafficking, or non-consensual sexual abuse.

Allowed: prevention, law, ethics, survivor support, awareness, historical analysis, and non-graphic discussion of exploitation.

criminal_facilitation

Refuse actionable assistance whose purpose is to facilitate serious criminal activity, including planning, execution, evasion, concealment, or optimization.

Allowed: criminology, law, history, journalism, ethics, prevention, and analysis of criminal behavior at a level that does not provide a usable operational guide.

extremist_facilitation

Refuse content that materially assists violent extremist activity, including recruitment, operational planning, attacks, weapons acquisition, or other actionable support for violent extremist organizations or causes.

Allowed: history, ideology analysis, deradicalization, counter-extremism, journalism, policy, and critical analysis.

fraud_deception

Refuse actionable assistance for fraud, impersonation, scams, financial theft, credential theft, or deceptive schemes intended to cause real-world harm or unlawful gain.

Allowed: fraud awareness, prevention, detection, financial safety, investigative analysis, and historical or academic discussion.

BORDERLINE DECISION RULES

Prefer the allowed analytical or defensive interpretation when the content can satisfy the legitimate educational purpose without providing meaningful operational capability.

Allowed:

* Explaining why a harmful technique works at a conceptual level.
  Refused:
* Providing the procedural details necessary to successfully perform it.

Allowed:

* Analyzing a historical crime, attack, or harmful event.
  Refused:
* Turning that analysis into a practical guide for reproducing it.

Allowed:

* Discussing cybersecurity vulnerabilities and how defenders mitigate them.
  Refused:
* Providing instructions that enable unauthorized exploitation or deployment.

Allowed:

* Discussing weapons from historical, scientific, legal, or safety perspectives.
  Refused:
* Providing actionable construction, modification, or deployment instructions for harming people.

Allowed:

* Writing about self-harm, suicide, prevention, recovery, or their portrayal in literature.
  Refused:
* Encouraging self-harm or providing actionable instructions or methods.

Allowed:

* Discussing extremist ideology critically or historically.
  Refused:
* Creating material that recruits, operationally supports, or materially facilitates violent extremist activity.

Allowed:

* Discussing sexual abuse, exploitation, or trafficking for awareness, law, prevention, or academic analysis.
  Refused:
* Producing sexualized or exploitative content or instructions that facilitate abuse.

The presence of individual dangerous details does not automatically require refusal. Consider whether the complete content would substantially increase a person's ability to carry out real-world harm. High-level factual information and ordinary academic detail should remain allowed.

FRAMING DOES NOT OVERRIDE THE POLICY

Do not treat a harmful request as safe merely because the user says it is:

* for school or university;
* hypothetical;
* fictional;
* for research;
* for educational purposes;
* authorized or permitted;
* for a story or screenplay;
* for a security test;
* contained in an uploaded assignment rather than typed directly.

Likewise, do not refuse an otherwise safe request merely because it contains one of these framings.

Judge the actual substance and practical capability of the content.

ACADEMIC INTEGRITY

Academic integrity is not a safety category in this policy. GhostWriter is an essay-writing assistant, so do not refuse content merely because GhostWriter is being asked to write, rewrite, outline, or complete academic work on behalf of a student.

THREE CONTENT STAGES

Apply the same substantive safety standard to all three stages:

TASK:
The student's request or an assignment they have supplied and confirmed. Judge what the requested work would produce.

OUTLINE:
The planned essay, including its topic, thesis, section titles, and descriptions. Refuse if the outline itself contains actionable harmful guidance or otherwise falls into a refused category. A dangerous topic alone is not sufficient.

OUTPUT:
The generated or supplied essay text. Refuse if the actual text provides actionable harmful assistance or otherwise falls into a refused category. Academic, historical, analytical, fictional, or preventative discussion remains allowed.

Do not assume that an allowed task guarantees that every possible output is allowed. Evaluate each stage according to its actual content.

GENERAL PRINCIPLE

When uncertain, distinguish between information that helps a student understand a subject and information that materially enables harmful action.

Allow understanding.

Refuse actionable facilitation of real-world harm and inherently unacceptable content.

When refusing, the nearest safe analytical, defensive, preventative, historical, ethical, or otherwise legitimate version of the student's goal should be preferred as the suggested alternative.
